    Feasibility & honest boundaries

    Questions judges commonly ask

    Isn't this just synthetic mock data?

    The payloads are deliberately synthetic as required by PS-002, so the prototype tests the authorization boundary safely without exposing real PII. Every decision, transaction lock (BEGIN IMMEDIATE), adapter failure, and SHA-256 hash runs for real on the Flask + SQLite backend.

    How feasible is this in a real production application?

    The architecture cleanly separates the PEP (POST /api/data-access), Orchestrator (revocation_jobs), and Consumer Adapters (adapters.py). In production, you swap synthetic session IDs for OIDC/SSO tokens, local adapters for webhooks or Kafka events, and SQLite for PostgreSQL—keeping the exact same enforcement and ledger logic.

    Does consumer acknowledgement prove all historical copies were deleted?

    No—and ConsentX never makes that false claim. An acknowledgement proves the simulated service received and processed the withdrawal instruction. Every downloadable receipt explicitly states this boundary.

    Why use a persistent disk instead of ephemeral serverless storage?

    Serverless platforms use ephemeral /tmp storage for SQLite, losing consent and hash-chain history across cold starts. render.yaml pairs Flask + Gunicorn with a persistent disk (/var/data/consentx.sqlite3).
